Abstract

Well-differentiated customer classification results serve as a critical basis for enterprises to integrate marketing resources and adjust marketing strategies. This paper extracts partial airline customer data based on the LRFMC model and uses the K-means clustering method to classify airline customers, resulting in five distinct customer groups. Customer characteristics are summarized and corresponding marketing recommendations are provided, with the aim of delivering personalized services and targeted marketing strategies to customer segments of varying value.

Keywords: Passenger classification; Data mining; LRFMC model; K-means clustering; Strategy analysis

1 Introduction

With the continuous advancement of technology and the advent of the big data era, data mining is increasingly becoming one of the core technical approaches for studying consumer behavior and characteristics. Clustering analysis theory provides an important theoretical foundation for data mining and machine learning. In enterprise decision-making across various domains, clustering analysis is commonly used for customer segmentation and user profiling, enabling businesses to accurately identify customer needs and deliver personalized recommendations and services. It is evident that cluster-based segmentation is an effective approach for market segmentation, customer segmentation, and consumer behavior research.

Traditional passenger classification methods primarily fall into two categories: one distinguishes passengers by flight type, dividing customers into business class and economy class travelers; the other segments customers based on accumulated flight mileage, where those meeting certain mileage thresholds are classified as VIP customers and the rest as ordinary customers. Both approaches are overly simplistic and fail to adequately reflect the diverse consumption characteristics of different customers. Factors such as airline promotional activities, customer airline preferences, and flight frequency are not taken into account, making these methods insufficient for meeting the demands of big data informatization. In recent years, some scholars have classified passengers based on their characteristics, primarily clustering by inherent customer information such as gender, age, occupation, and income. However, against the backdrop of the big data era and informatization development, customer characteristics tend to become more complex, rendering such methods inadequate for comprehensive customer profiling.

Therefore, this paper adopts the LRFMC model — which provides more comprehensive coverage of customer profiling characteristics — to extract partial airline data, and employs the K-means clustering method to classify airline customers into different groups, summarize their characteristics, and provide a reference for delivering personalized services and formulating targeted marketing strategies for customer segments of varying value.

2 Literature Review

2.1 Customer Value Segmentation

Hu Hai (2022) proposed using the K-means algorithm to improve the RFM model by incorporating quantified RFM attribute values and the K-means algorithm into RS theory (LEM2 algorithm), effectively addressing corresponding deficiencies to better focus on target customers. Yang Jiaxin (2022) used the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to calculate the weight of each attribute and constructed an improved LFMN model based on the traditional RFM model for user value segmentation. Yan Chun et al. (2020) introduced the customer claim amount indicator to establish an RFMC model and dynamically adjusted the training speed and weight vectors of the SOM neural network model, improving the model convergence speed by 21.6% and achieving customer segmentation. Li Weikang et al. (2020) established an improved RVMF model based on the RFM model and applied AHP to optimize the classification of existing customers.

It is evident that existing research on customer segmentation predominantly uses the RFM model, or selects corresponding value indicators based on the RFM model according to actual industry conditions, incorporating weight calculation methods such as AHP — all of which possess industry-specific targeting.

2.2 Customer Clustering Algorithms

Yan Chun et al. (2020) introduced the customer claim amount indicator and dynamically adjusted the training speed and weight vectors of the SOM neural network model, improving convergence speed by 21.6%. Wang Changqiong (2018) used a spectral clustering algorithm combined with the empirical rule k ≤ √n to calculate the maximum eigenvalue difference of the Laplace matrix to determine the number of customer clusters. Han Shilian (2016) proposed a fuzzy systematic clustering method that reflects multiple customer demand attributes for customer classification, applying intelligent weighting to integrate dynamic attributes and generate corresponding distribution strategies. Zhu Yuanhai et al. (2009) combined the PSO algorithm with the mean clustering algorithm to optimize the total within-class dispersion to its minimum, thereby obtaining optimal customer clusters. Ye Miaoqun (2005) proposed an improved K-medoids clustering algorithm to analyze Web user behavior, using fuzzy similarity and mimicking fitness calculation ideas from genetic algorithms to improve upon the local optimization characteristics of traditional clustering algorithms.

Due to the varying selection of customer characteristic indicator attributes, domestic scholars employ different clustering algorithms, all optimizing and improving upon traditional algorithms such as spectral clustering, hierarchical clustering, mean clustering, and K-medoids clustering — each with strong domain-specific targeting.

3 Research Content

This paper proposes using the LRFMC model to describe customer characteristics. The model provides relatively comprehensive coverage of airline customer features and represents a viable approach for user feature extraction. The K-means clustering model is used for customer category analysis, treating observations in the data as objects with positions and inter-object distances. Rather than applying weighting and processing operations to customer indicators, data indicators of different dimensions are directly standardized and then clustered, yielding a practical classification result through simplified processing.

In the context of big data, the analysis of user behavior and characteristics is conducted by detecting user group behavior to obtain data for holistic analysis. Here, user behavior information may include all information collected from user actions during transactions, as well as inherent individual characteristic information such as gender and age. For airlines, customer characteristics include basic customer information, customer consumption information, and customer loyalty points information. How to aggregate and categorize these characteristics forms an important foundation for airline customer segmentation. The main research content of this paper is as follows:

  1. Data Preprocessing
  2. Customer Feature Indicator Extraction
  3. Customer Clustering Process
  4. Category Characteristic Summarization

Classifying customers based on airline data falls under unsupervised learning. A clustering mining model is adopted, using a distance-based approach between individuals and clusters to iteratively classify customers. Before solving the clustering model, it is necessary to select corresponding indicators for the target profile — the selection of indicators must be determined in conjunction with actual business conditions and must reflect the key characteristics of customers. Key features are selected based on data processing results, followed by clustering model solving.

Based on the final clustering results, different customer categories are analyzed for their characteristics, comparing the features and value of different customer segments. Corresponding marketing strategies are formulated for customers of varying value, providing them with personalized services.

4 Research Approach

User behavior analysis enables a more detailed understanding of user behavioral habits, thereby identifying problems in the marketing environment such as website and promotional channel issues. This allows product marketing to become more precise, effective, and ultimately enhance enterprise revenue. Clustering analysis can group samples with similar characteristics into the same cluster, and further analyze samples within the same cluster to obtain their characteristic distribution.

This paper proposes using clustering to segment customers into categories, grouping customers with a high degree of similarity into the same category, and conducting visual analysis based on clustering results to mine the purchasing behaviors of different customer categories. This enables more accurate identification of customer needs and the adoption of corresponding marketing strategies, providing enterprises with a theoretical basis for maximizing benefits.

The LRFMC model is adopted for user feature extraction. Traditionally, consumer behavior characteristic analysis has often employed the RFM model. In the RFM model, monetary value represents the total amount of products purchased by a customer from the enterprise over a period of time, which has general applicability to existing consumer systems. However, against the backdrop of the current big data era, airline ticket prices are influenced by factors such as transport distance and cabin class, and airline-customer related parameters such as time and flight frequency exhibit diversity — meaning that customers with higher monetary value are not necessarily more valuable than those with lower monetary value, reflecting industry specificity. Moreover, airlines periodically issue discount coupons offering reduced fares to existing customers, making the traditional RFM model insufficient for processing current airline data.

Based on this, this paper uses the cumulative flight mileage (M) over a period and the average discount coefficient (C) of cabin classes taken over a period to represent monetary value. The customer relationship length (L) is then added to the model, forming the LRFMC model.

The K-means clustering model is used for customer classification, treating observations in the data as objects with positions and inter-object distances. It partitions objects into K mutually exclusive clusters, making objects within each cluster as close to each other as possible while remaining as far as possible from objects in other clusters. This paper directly calls the K-means function from the pyspark.ml.clustering library, where each cluster’s characteristics are determined by its centroid or center point, and distance is calculated using Euclidean distance metric. Given the large volume of data, no weighting is applied to customer indicator calculations; instead, data indicators of different dimensions are directly standardized and then clustered to achieve higher clustering computational efficiency.

5.3.2 Correlation Analysis

The magnitude of covariance alone does not adequately measure the degree of association between two random variables, as its value is influenced by the dimensions of both variables and is thus unsuitable for comparison. To better measure the degree of correlation between two random variables, the Pearson correlation coefficient is introduced, which divides the covariance by the standard deviations of both random variables, eliminating dimensional influence.

Pearson Correlation Coefficient Formula:

$$ r_{x,y} = \frac{\sum{(X-\bar{X})(Y-\bar{Y})}}{\sqrt{\sum{(X-\bar{X})^2} \cdot \sum{(Y-\bar{Y})^2}}} $$

This paper uses the scipy library’s corr method to calculate Pearson correlation coefficients, analyzing the correlations among the five selected LRFMC attributes.

The pairplot analysis reveals a linear correlation relationship between the F and M features.

Further exploration of feature correlations via the heatmap shows that the F and M features have a correlation coefficient of 0.83 (>0.8), indicating a very strong linear relationship.

6 Modeling Process

6.1 Model Preparation

6.1.1 Notation

A. Unlabeled Dataset:

$$ X = \begin{bmatrix} x^{(1)} \ x^{(2)} \ \dots \ x^{(m)} \end{bmatrix} $$

B. Minimized Loss Function:

$$ E = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{x\in C_i} |x - \mu_i|^2 $$

C. Cluster Center Point:

$$ \mu_i = \frac{1}{|C_i|}\sum_{x \in C_i} x $$

6.2 Model Establishment

K-means is one of the most widely used partition-based unsupervised classification algorithms. It partitions n objects into k clusters, aiming for high similarity within clusters. Similarity is calculated based on the mean of objects within a cluster.

The K-means algorithm adopts an iterative update approach: first, k objects are randomly selected, each representing the initial mean or center of a cluster. For each remaining object, based on its Euclidean distance to each cluster center, it is assigned to the most similar cluster. Then, the K-means algorithm iteratively improves the within-cluster variation. For each cluster, it uses the objects assigned to that cluster in the previous iteration to calculate a new mean. The updated mean is then used as the new cluster center, and all objects are reassigned. Iteration continues until the assignment stabilizes — i.e., the clusters formed in the current round are identical to those formed in the previous round.

Specific Steps:

  1. Randomly select k sample points to serve as the center points of each cluster {μ₁, μ₂, …, μₖ}
  2. Calculate the distances between all sample points and each cluster center dist(xⁱ, μⱼ), then assign sample points to the nearest cluster:

$$ dist(x^{(i)}, \mu_j) = \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{n}(x^{(i)}t - \mu{jt})^2} $$

  1. Recalculate cluster centers μ_new based on existing sample points in each cluster:

$$ \mu_i = \frac{1}{|C_i|}\sum_{x \in C_i} x $$

  1. Repeat steps 2 and 3

Model Processing Flow:

  • Input: Number of clusters k; dataset X containing n objects X = {x₁, x₂, …, xₙ}
  • Output: k clusters {S₁, S₂, …, Sₖ}

6.3.2 Customer Clustering

Determining the Number of Clusters:

This paper uses the elbow method to determine the number of clusters, using SSE (Sum of Squared Errors) as the indicator:

$$ SSE = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{p\in C_i} |p - m_i|^2 $$

Where Cᵢ is the i-th cluster, mᵢ is the i-th centroid, and p is a data point belonging to Cᵢ.

6.4 Model Improvement Analysis

6.4.1 Model Advantages

LRFMC Customer Feature Model: The M feature in the original RFM model is replaced by cumulative flight mileage (M) over a period and the average discount coefficient (C) of cabin classes taken over a period. Additionally, membership enrollment time is considered to influence customer value to some extent, with customer relationship length (L) added as a distinguishing feature. Compared to the traditional RFM model, this provides more comprehensive customer feature coverage and is more suitable for customer characteristic analysis specific to the airline industry.

Clustering Algorithm Model: The K-means algorithm is simple, intuitive, and produces good clustering results. The elbow method is used to reasonably determine the K value, enhancing model accuracy. For the airline customer data analyzed in this paper — 62,988 source records and 3,586 records after cleaning — K-means clustering on large datasets offers advantages of fast convergence and high clustering efficiency compared to traditional hierarchical clustering.

6.4.2 Model Limitations

Since K-Means clustering is an iterative algorithm whose results are affected by initial values and represents a local optimum, it is influenced by the selection of initial cluster center points. If initial cluster centers happen to be outlier attribute points, the overall customer clustering classification results will be affected. The algorithm continuously iterates to partition data center points, and noise and outliers can interfere with the distance calculations for center partitioning.

In terms of customer feature attribute selection, the model does not yet consider the cancellation rate caused by uncertain factors such as weather and itinerary for airline customers. The cancellation rate is also an important indicator for airlines studying customer quality, so the model still requires further improvement.

6.4.3 Model Improvements

1. Cluster Center Selection Optimization:

Existing research on K-Means clustering algorithm improvements includes approaches such as the K-Means++ algorithm. When selecting the (n+1)-th cluster center, points farther from the current n cluster centers have a higher probability of being selected as the (n+1)-th cluster center, optimizing K-Means random centroid initialization. The bisecting K-Means algorithm first treats all data as one cluster, then performs cluster partitioning, ensuring that the overall error at each step is minimized.

2. Distance Calculation Optimization:

The elkan K-Means algorithm leverages the triangle inequality properties (the sum of two sides is greater than or equal to the third side, and the difference between two sides is less than the third side) to reduce distance calculations. For a sample point and two centroids, the first approach pre-calculates the distance between the two centroids — if twice the distance from the sample point to one centroid is less than the distance to the other centroid, the sample point directly belongs to the cluster of the first centroid. The second approach uses triangle properties to obtain the centroid with the shortest distance to the sample. Optimizing the distance calculation of the K-Means algorithm accelerates the iteration speed.

7.3 Marketing Recommendations

7.3.1 Membership Tier Management

Based on the customer clustering results, airline members can be divided into multiple different types and assigned different membership tiers. By calculating scores according to certain criteria, members can be classified into ordinary members and core members. Becoming a core member requires accumulating a certain amount of flight mileage within a specified period. Upon meeting the score requirements, members become core members within the validity period and enjoy corresponding premium services. As the validity period approaches its end, relevant evaluation methods determine whether the customer qualifies to continue as a core member, followed by appropriate tier upgrades or downgrades.

Additionally, promotions can be implemented on this basis. Before the membership tier evaluation period, the airline can appropriately remind high-consumption customers who are close to but have not yet met the requirements, and implement accelerated upgrade or promotional activities to stimulate their consumption to reach the corresponding indicators. This not only generates revenue but also improves customer satisfaction and expands the enterprise’s core member base.

7.3.2 Points Redemption Incentives

In corporate marketing processes, first-time redemption or free gifts are commonly used to attract customers. In formulating airline marketing strategies, first-time redemption can be enabled only when customer-end mileage or flight segments accumulate to a certain level. Data analysis reveals that the airline’s customer enthusiasm for points redemption is not high. Points-based ticket discounts can be used to attract customers, stimulating their loyalty to the airline and improving service satisfaction.

On the other hand, there may be an information gap issue where some customers are unaware of the points redemption model. Measures the airline can take include extracting members from the database who are close to but have not yet met the first-time redemption standards and providing them with SMS or phone call reminders or promotions.

7.3.3 Co-branded and Bundled Sales

Measures such as issuing co-branded cards with banks, offering points for phone bill top-ups, and collaborating with other e-commerce platforms to issue ticket packages can improve customer loyalty. This enables customers to earn the company’s points through consumption with other enterprises, strengthening their connection with the company, expanding the company’s business coverage, increasing the airline’s visibility in other domains, and enhancing customer loyalty. Increasing user stickiness, maintaining customer relationships, and maximizing the interactive value between the company and customers throughout the lifecycle.
