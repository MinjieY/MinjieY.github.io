Loan Default Risk Assessment Based on ANN Neural Networks — A Case Study of Lending Club Data

Abstract

This paper conducts an in-depth study on credit risk prediction in the P2P lending market. By analyzing and mining Lending Club’s loan data from 2007 to 2020, totaling 2,925,493 records with 142 fields, we propose an attribute classification method based on loan application information combined with customer segmentation.

During data preprocessing, we ensure data quality through null and duplicate row removal, outlier elimination, unit/symbol stripping, and normalization. Feature engineering (feature derivation, abstraction, and scaling) further optimizes the data. Using the Keras Tuner for hyperparameter optimization, we establish an ANN model achieving 84.52% accuracy, significantly outperforming logistic regression (56.81%) and XGBoost (64.94%).

Keywords: Data Mining; Lending; Risk Assessment; ANN; Artificial Neural Networks

1. Research Background & Literature Review

1.1 Background

In traditional loan decision-making, financial institutions primarily rely on borrowers’ credit reports, occupation, and income to assess repayment ability. However, this information often fails to fully reflect borrowers’ true risk profiles. Data mining techniques can capture multi-dimensional borrower data and perform in-depth analysis of historical transactions and behavioral trajectories.

Lending Club, founded in 2007, was one of the largest and most renowned P2P lending platforms in the US. Its publicly released dataset covering 2.92 million Americans across 13 years contains rich information including age, income, occupation, geographic location, loan purpose, and credit ratings — making it an ideal dataset for machine learning experiments.

1.2 Literature Review

Current P2P lending research focuses on four areas: operational models, transaction behavior, lending risk, and social networks. Most scholars approach risk prediction from a one-way consumer information perspective, but comprehensively accounting for multi-faceted real-world influences remains challenging.

In terms of credit risk evaluation modeling, combined qualitative-quantitative approaches and multi-type complex system modeling methods are widely applied in default risk assessment, with various machine learning methods representing the current development trend.

1.3 Paper Structure

2. Related Theory & Methods

2.1 Data Mining in Loan Decision-Making

Data mining helps financial institutions more accurately identify potential high-quality and poor customers. By analyzing massive datasets, data mining algorithms can discover hidden patterns and predict borrowers’ repayment ability and willingness more precisely, enabling more reasonable loan strategies.

2.2 Artificial Neural Networks (ANN)

This study employs the ANN algorithm, which models complex relationships between data by simulating the synaptic nodes of biological neural networks.

Neuron Structure

A neuron (activation unit) typically has multiple input values with weights, a bias term, and an output value y:

$$y = g\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i x_i + b\right)$$

where $x_i$ represents inputs, $w_i$ represents weights, and $b$ is the bias.

The ANN’s basic structure includes an input layer, hidden layers, and an output layer. The input layer receives raw data, hidden layers process the data, and the output layer produces final predictions.

2.3 Softmax Activation Function

Softmax is typically used in the output layer for multi-class classification problems, normalizing a vector of values into a probability distribution that sums to 1.

For the i-th node output:

$$\text{Softmax}(z_i) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{c=1}^{C} e^{z_c}}$$

where $z_i$ is the output value of the i-th node and C is the number of classes.

2.4 Cross-Entropy Loss Function

Cross-entropy loss measures the difference between predicted and true probability distributions in classification tasks:

$$L = -\sum_{c=1}^{M} y_{ic} \log(p_{ic})$$

where M is the number of classes, $y_{ic}$ is an indicator function, and $p_{ic}$ is the predicted probability.

2.5 Random Forest

Random Forest is an extended parallel ensemble learning method that uses bootstrap sampling to add sample perturbation and introduces attribute perturbation by randomly selecting a subset of K attributes at each split.

3. Data Collection & Preprocessing

3.1 Data Source

The dataset comes from Lending Club’s official website, containing loan information from 2007 to 2020: 2,925,493 records with 142 fields covering borrower demographics, loan details, and repayment status.

3.2 Data Preprocessing

Independent Variable Processing: Eliminated features unrelated to loan application and disbursement, retaining 18 observation variables. Removed null values and outliers from continuous variables.

Dependent Variable Processing: Remapped loan_status into three categories:

  • Paid (fully repaid): 86.8%
  • Default: 12.3%
  • Late (delinquent): 0.9%

3.3 Descriptive Statistics

Key findings from descriptive statistics:

  • Loan amount standard deviation of 9,458.46 indicates significant individual variation
  • Median loan term: 36 months (SD: 11.04 months)
  • Median interest rate: 12.49% (SD: 4.86%), relatively concentrated distribution

Most customers have monthly payments between 0-500, loan amounts peaking around 10,000, and debt-to-income ratios under 50%. Loan terms show pronounced extremes at 36 and 60 months.

3.4 Multicollinearity Test (VIF)

VIF quantifies the degree of multicollinearity between independent variables:

$$\text{VIF}_i = \frac{1}{1 - R_i^2}$$

Variables with VIF > 10 indicate high multicollinearity and will be addressed through PCA dimensionality reduction.

3.5 Cross-Validation & Imbalanced Data Handling

The dataset is highly imbalanced, requiring cross-validation to reduce model evaluation variance.

Comparison of sampling methods:

Method Paid Acc. Default Acc. Late Acc. Overall Acc.
Under-sampling High Medium Low 13%
Over-sampling Low 0.88 Low 88%

Random over-sampling outperforms under-sampling, achieving excellent Default class metrics (Precision: 0.9, Recall: 0.97, F1: 0.93).

4. Variable Selection & Feature Engineering

4.1 Variable Selection

Features are categorized into two dimensions:

Loan Information: Monthly installment, debt level, loan amount, interest rate, loan grade

Customer Information:

  • Hard metrics: Annual income, debt-to-income ratio, housing situation
  • Personal factors: Employment length, loan purpose
  • Credit indicators: Delinquency count, open credit accounts, derogatory public records, revolving credit balance, income verification, revolving credit utilization

4.2 Feature Derivation — PCA Dimensionality Reduction

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to highly collinear loan information features.

The k-th principal component contribution rate:

$$\phi_k = \frac{\lambda_k}{\sum_{i=1}^{p} \lambda_i}$$

Factor loading:

$$l_{ik} = \sqrt{\lambda_k} \cdot e_{ik}$$

3 principal components explain over 95% of variance, capturing 60.56%, 22.10%, and 16.50% respectively — totaling 99.16% of variance.

4.3 Feature Abstraction

One-Hot Encoding: Converted categorical text features (home ownership, identity verification, loan purpose) into binary vectors.

Numerical Mapping: Mapped Lending Club’s A-G grade system (each with 1-5 sub-grades) into continuous numerical scores for quantification.

4.4 Feature Scaling — Normalization

Applied min-max normalization to eliminate dimensional differences:

$$x_{norm} = \frac{x - x_{min}}{x_{max} - x_{min}}$$

5. Model Construction & Evaluation

5.1 Model Architecture

Hyperparameter tuning via Keras Tuner:

Parameter Search Range
Hidden Layers 4 ~ 20
Neurons per Layer 32 ~ 512 (step 32)
Learning Rate 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001
Output Activation Softmax
Loss Function Categorical Cross-Entropy

Optimal configuration: 3 dense layers with 288, 320, and 64 neurons.

5.2 Model Performance

Overall accuracy: 84.52%

Metric Value
Accuracy 84.52%
Precision 81.04%
Recall 84.52%
F1 Score 82.40%

5.3 Model Comparison

Model Accuracy
ANN 84.52%
XGBoost 64.94%
Logistic Regression 56.81%

The ANN model significantly outperforms both XGBoost and logistic regression on this dataset.

5.4 Model Advantages

  1. Tuning Flexibility: Keras Tuner enables efficient hyperparameter search
  2. Probabilistic Output: Softmax activation provides probability distributions
  3. Effective Loss Function: Categorical cross-entropy accurately measures prediction error
  4. Multi-Metric Evaluation: Comprehensive assessment using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score

6. Feature Analysis & Business Recommendations

6.1 Key Feature Identification

The most discriminative features for loan categories are:

  • term (loan duration)
  • open_acc (open credit accounts)
  • int_rate (interest rate)
  • sub_grade (loan sub-grade)
  • revol_bal (revolving credit balance)
  • installment (monthly payment)

6.2 Customer Profiles & Recommendations

Paid Customers (Fully Repaid)

Characteristics: Significantly lower interest rates, loan sub-grades, and monthly payments. These customers have good credit history and repayment ability.

Recommendations:

  • Lower loan thresholds and simplify application processes
  • Increase lending volume to achieve economies of scale
  • Offer preferential policies (fee waivers, rate reductions)

Late Customers (Delinquent Repayment)

Characteristics: Lower platform ratings, higher monthly payments and interest rates, greater repayment pressure.

Recommendations:

  • Conduct regular/real-time monitoring
  • Arrange repayment reminders
  • Adjust repayment terms and monthly payments based on circumstances
  • Strengthen post-loan management

Default Customers (Non-Repayment)

Characteristics: Relatively balanced features with the highest interest rates, indicating elevated risk.

Recommendations:

  • Strengthen risk assessment and feature weight allocation
  • Require additional identity verification or cross-platform binding
  • Collaborate with other institutions to freeze/restrict defaulting accounts
  • Develop effective collection strategies
  • Account for potential bad debt losses

7. Conclusions & Future Work

7.1 Conclusions

Based on Lending Club’s 2.92 million loan records (2007-2020), this study established an ANN risk assessment model through a complete data mining pipeline:

  1. Data Preprocessing: Null/duplicate removal, outlier elimination, unit stripping via regex, normalization
  2. Feature Engineering: PCA reduction (6 features → 3 components, 99.16% variance), one-hot encoding, numerical mapping, normalization
  3. Imbalance Handling: Over-sampling accuracy (88%) far exceeds under-sampling (13%)
  4. Model Performance: ANN achieves 84.52% accuracy, significantly outperforming XGBoost (64.94%) and logistic regression (56.81%)

7.2 Limitations

  1. Data Scope: Sample population is US citizens; results may differ for East Asian markets
  2. Environmental Changes: The 13-year span includes inflation, policy shifts, and COVID-19 behavioral impacts
  3. Imbalance Methods: Only random over-sampling and distance-based under-sampling were compared; more approaches exist

7.3 Future Directions

  • Explore ensemble methods: Stacking, Bagging, Boosting
  • Automated feature selection and construction
  • Multi-modal data integration (images, audio, IP addresses)
  • Third-party consumption data incorporation
  • Explainable models for feature-driven customer classification and risk quantification
